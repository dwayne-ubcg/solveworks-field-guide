# SolveWorks Pre-Call Briefing — Red-Team Review

## 1. Verdict: **SEND with light revisions**

This is genuinely strong, differentiated copy. The "operating layer around the agent" positioning is clear, consistent, and lands. The weaknesses are minor and concentrated in a few spots. None are send-blockers, but the fixes below tighten it materially.

---

## 2. Weak / Generic / Awkward Copy

**Page 3 — "We build agents like operators, not toys."**
"Toys" is the weakest word in the deck. It undercuts your premium positioning by introducing a juvenile frame. Prospects don't think they're considering "toys."

**Page 4 — "an agent that sounds impressive once"**
Slightly clunky phrasing. The idea is right; the line drags.

**Page 1 subhead — "The head start your agent gets when SolveWorks builds it."**
"When SolveWorks builds it" is awkward as a hanging clause. Fine, but not as tight as the rest.

**Page 9 — four-quadrant + closing line** is the most generic page (expected for a bio), but "purpose-built" and "the way a company actually operates" repeat phrasing used elsewhere. Mild fatigue, not a problem.

---

## 3. Replacement Copy (only where materially stronger)

**Page 3 headline:**
> ~~We build agents like operators, not toys.~~
> **We build agents like operators, not demos.**

(Keeps your contrast intact — "demo" is the actual category your prospects fear buying, and it ties back to your Page 7 "not a generic demo" line.)

**Page 4 closing line:**
> ~~This is the difference between an agent that sounds impressive once and an agent that can become part of daily operations.~~
> **This is the difference between an agent that impresses in a demo and one that earns a place in daily operations.**

**Page 1 subhead (optional polish):**
> **The head start your agent gets the day SolveWorks builds it.**

---

## 4. Claims to Soften or Prove

**Page 2 — "Since December, the biggest lesson from real agent builds…"**
This is your one explicit proof anchor and it's doing a lot of work. "Since December" reads thin/under-specified — it raises the question "how many builds?" without answering. Either:
- Add a quiet quantifier if you have it ("across [N] real builds since December"), **or**
- Reframe so the authority comes from the *pattern* rather than the timeline: **"Every real agent build since December has confirmed one thing:"**

As written, it's a credible claim but currently leans on a date that invites scrutiny rather than supplying confidence.

**Page 5 — "This is why our builds compound. Each real workflow teaches the system how to do the next one better."**
This is a strong claim presented as established fact. If you have even one example of reuse across two builds, it's defensible. If not, soften to a design intent: **"Our builds are designed to compound — each workflow makes the next one faster to stand up."** Don't claim an outcome you can't point to on a call.

**Page 3 — "memory"** appears in the workbench list but isn't a named layer anywhere else (Pages 4/8 use "Feedback Loops"). Minor inconsistency; align vocabulary to avoid a prospect asking "what's the memory product?"

---

## 5. Does It Set SolveWorks Apart? **Yes — clearly.**

The differentiation is the strongest part of this piece:
- The repeated "operating layer around the agent" frame is consistent across all 10 pages.
- The four failure modes on Page 2 (cold starts, no source truth, unsafe permissions, lost corrections) do real work — they make the prospect feel a problem they recognize but couldn't name.
- "Start read-only or draft-only" appears on Pages 3, 6, 8, and 10 — this safety posture is a genuine wedge against the "AI everywhere" hype crowd and reassures risk-averse owners.

**One gap:** the deck tells the prospect *what* SolveWorks builds extremely well, but the "head start" promise (Pages 1 and 8) is asserted more than it is *felt*. The most concrete proof of head start would be a single before/after line — e.g., what the team stops re-explaining after week one. Consider adding one tangible "day one vs. week one" contrast on Page 8 if you can support it. Not required to send.
